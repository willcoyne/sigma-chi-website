1 · Summary

No new framework. A new layer.

The effect fits the site's current stack. Vite, React 19 and Motion already handle scroll on the live hero; the missing piece is a 3D renderer for the table and die. After the first round of ten mockups, your feedback asked for something more realistic, more modern and glowier, with far less scrolling. Mockup 11 does that, mockup 12 is the same build ending in a cup, 13 and 14 add a holographic player who throws it (14 with a heavyweight build), and this plan builds toward them.

KeepVite 8, React 19, TypeScript, react-router 7 and Motion 13. Motion detects the first scroll and runs the intro clock.
Addthree through React Three Fiber 9, drei 10 and @react-three/postprocessing for bloom, lazy-loaded as one chunk on the home page only.
SkipGSAP, runtime physics engines, Spline and a framework migration. Each one duplicates Motion, needs a CSP change, or adds weight without changing the result.

The brief, as mockup 11 builds it

  • It loads looking normal. The first frame is today's hero: same headline, standfirst, buttons, pinstripes and crest watermark. The table is there, seen straight down and lit flat, so it reads as the familiar navy field.
  • One scroll starts it. The first bit of scroll, or a click on the cue, plays the whole sequence on its own clock in about 3.4 seconds. Nobody has to scroll four screens to see it. Scrolling back to the top rewinds it.
  • The background turns out to be the table. The camera cranes up from overhead to a player's view while gold light strips trace the inlay and center line, and four glass cups rise at the corners.
  • The crest becomes the die and gets thrown. The watermark flies onto the die's top face. The toss uses real gravity: about 5 ft of air, two bounces that lose energy, and a damped settle that always ends crest-up. "Toss again" throws it back from the other end, as players alternate in a real game.
  • Same palette. Every color comes from src/styles/theme.css; gold is the only color that glows. Type stays Cormorant Garamond and Source Sans 3, plus JetBrains Mono for the small HUD readouts.

2 · Fourteen mockups

Ten ways to throw it, then four more

Each mockup is one self-contained page in beer-die-redesign/mockups/, also published as a private artifact. The first ten each test a different reveal and, where useful, a different technology. Number 11 is the revision after your feedback, 12 is 11 with the die going into a cup, 13 adds a holographic player who throws it, and 14 gives him a heavyweight build. The large frame is each concept's signature moment; all of them open on today's hero, except 10, which never leaves it.

11Glow Toss Revision · recommended

The direction after your feedback. It has physically based materials, neon gold inlays, glass cups, bloom, and a HUD that reads out the die's height and spin. One small scroll plays the whole toss in about 3.4 seconds with real ballistics, and "Toss again" throws it back the other way.

Built with three.js 0.160, bloom post-processing and a custom studio environment map. Triggered by scroll but runs on its own clock; pinned for under one screen.

12Cup Sink Variant of 11

11 with a different ending. The die clears the rim on a gravity arc, drops in, rattles against the wall and settles crest-up, and the cup answers with a gold flash, a short column of light and sparks. "Toss again" alternates ends and works round all four cups.

Built with the same stack as 11. The cups are drawn 8 in across and the die a little smaller so it fits inside.

13Holo Thrower 12 with a player

A featureless figure drawn only in blue contour lines prints itself in from the floor at the near end, through falling blue code. It lobs the die underhand, watches it, and pumps a fist when it sinks. The camera ends side-on, so the swing reads in profile and the arc crosses the frame.

Built with the same stack as 12. The figure is generated in code, with no model file: lofted rings, a joint hierarchy and leg IK.

14Jacked Thrower 13, heavyweight

13 with a heavyweight build: broad shoulders and lats over a narrow waist, pecs, a six-pack, big delts and arms, quads and diamond calves. Brighter lines trace the muscle borders. The camera ends on a front three-quarter view so the build faces you, and the arc still crosses to the cups.

Built with the same code as 13. Each cross-section is an ellipse swollen by Gaussian muscles, and the proportions are a table of numbers.

01Tilt Reveal

The hero's navy field tilts back and turns out to be the painted table. The crest watermark flies onto the die's top face, and the die arcs over the line and lands crest-up.

Built with CSS 3D transforms and vanilla JS, scrubbed by scroll over 3.6 screens.

02Cross Fold

The crest's White Cross drops onto the table as six squares, because an unfolded die is a cross. The squares fold up on hinges into the die, which is then thrown.

Built with CSS 3D hinges and vanilla JS, scrubbed over 4.2 screens.

03Overhead Toss

The camera stays straight overhead while the page zooms out to a 4 × 8 ft table. The toss reads through the die's growing scale and a shadow that slides away and softens.

Built with one SVG and 2D transforms, no 3D at all, scrubbed over 3.4 screens. The cheapest and most robust option.

04Altitude

A side view drawn to scale. The view climbs with the die past a ruler (table top 30 in, head height 6 ft, apex 10 ft), and the die drops into the far cup.

Built with GSAP 3.12 and ScrollTrigger, pinned and scrubbed over 4.5 screens.

05Six Faces

The crest becomes face 1 of a large pinned die. Each chapter of the page turns it to its next face while the table rises behind it out of focus, and the last chapter throws it onto the table.

Built with Motion's scroll() and animate() and a CSS 3D die, scrubbed over 7 screens.

06Pull Back

The hero shrinks into a rounded square that turns out to be the die's top face. The camera pulls back to the die on the table, then to the whole table, and then the die is thrown.

Built with a log-space 2D zoom and a CSS 3D die, scrubbed over 3.8 screens.

07Tablecloth Pull

The hero is a navy cloth over the table. Scrolling yanks it off sideways while the cups wobble in place, and the crest printed on it stays behind as the die.

Built with CSS 3D, skew and an SVG displacement filter, scrubbed over 4 screens.

08Split Stage

The page reads normally on the left while a sticky panel on the right holds the table, and the toss follows reading progress. On phones the panel becomes a band under the nav.

Built with Motion scroll() with two targets and a CSS 3D table. No extra scroll; it rides along with the page.

093D Table

The first WebGL build: a lit, rounded die, a painted table on legs, lathe-turned cups and soft shadows. The camera cranes from straight down to a player's view.

Built with three.js 0.160 through an importmap, scrubbed over 3.6 screens.

10Quiet Version

Today's home page, nearly untouched. The nav crest becomes a small die that tumbles with reading progress, and a small table at the end catches it beside the Join button.

Built with native CSS scroll-driven animations with a small JS fallback. No libraries and no extra scroll.

Recommendation: build 11 or 12. They're one build with two endings: 11 lands crest-up on the table and 12 sinks the die in a cup. Picking one is a matter of taste, and the code could even ship both, with the ending chosen per toss. Either one is the brief made the way you asked for in the second round: realistic, glowing, and started by one flick of the wheel instead of four screens of scrolling. 13 and 14 show that a thrower can be layered onto either ending with no change to the stack; 14's build is the same code with different numbers. Section 8 lists what every effect needs. Two first-round ideas are worth keeping in reserve. 02's cross fold is an alternative crest-to-die moment that ties the die to the White Cross. 10's nav die could carry the theme onto the other pages without a second 3D scene.

Why 11 plays on its own clock

Mockups 01 to 09 scrub: every pixel of scroll moves the scene a little, so the toss lasts as long as the reader keeps scrolling (three to seven screens). That's what made them feel like a lot of mouse work. 11 is scroll-triggered instead. The first downward scroll starts a fixed-length timeline, and the reader can keep scrolling, or not, while it plays. It's also why the toss can look physical: gravity needs real seconds, not scroll pixels. The cost is that the reader no longer controls the speed, so the sequence is kept under five seconds (the WCAG 2.2.2 threshold for moving content), it can be skipped, and reduced motion jumps straight to the landed frame.

5 · Architecture

One clock, two layers

The stage is a short section, 175vh, with a sticky viewport. Motion watches the scroll position. The first downward scroll animates an intro clock from 0 to 1.6 seconds, and returning to the top animates it back to 0 at double speed. Pure functions turn that clock, plus the current toss's own clock, into every pose. The HTML layer (headline, crest watermark, HUD, closing card) and the 3D scene read the same values, so they can't drift apart.

6 · Choreography

The toss, second by second

Timings from mockup 11. The intro runs 1.6 seconds; the toss is released at 1.3 seconds and has its own clock, so "Toss again" can replay it without the intro. The scene is built in feet, so gravity is 32.2 ft/s².

Bounce heights are 0.5 ft and 0.1 ft, so they last 2√(2h/g): 0.35 s and 0.16 s. The settle is a 0.45 s damped wobble onto a whole number of turns, which is what guarantees crest-up. Impacts fire a gold ring on the table and a tiny camera shake. Mockup 12 shares the intro; its flight (1.1 s) ends just over a cup's rim, followed by a 0.16 s drop inside, a small hop off the bottom and a 0.55 s rattle that settles crest-up.

The core of it in code

// useIntroClock.ts: one scroll plays it, the top of the page rewinds it
export function useIntroClock() {
  const tau = useMotionValue(0);
  const lastTarget = useRef(0);
  const reduce = useReducedMotion();
  const { scrollY } = useScroll();
  useMotionValueEvent(scrollY, "change", (y) => {
    const target = y > 6 ? INTRO_END : 0;
    if (target === lastTarget.current) return;
    lastTarget.current = target;
    const distance = Math.abs(target - tau.get());
    animate(tau, target, { ease: "linear", duration: reduce ? 0 : target ? distance : distance / 2 });
  });
  return tau;
}

// tossPhysics.ts: closed-form, deterministic, always lands crest-up
export function tossState(k: Toss, t: number): TossState {
  if (t < k.flight) {
    const u = t / k.flight;                       // apex = G * flight² / 8 ≈ 5.3 ft
    return { ...lerp2(k.from, k.p1, u), height: 4 * k.apex * u * (1 - u), angle: k.A1 * u, phase: phaseOf(u) };
  }
  // ...two shorter parabolas for the bounces, then a damped settle onto A4 = 3 full turns
}

7 · The die and the table

Realistic materials, one glowing color

Die

  • RoundedBox with 6 segments and a 15% edge radius.
  • MeshPhysicalMaterial: navy lacquer, clearcoat 1. Three canvases per face: colour (gold pips, the crest on face 1), glow (emissive, so the pips bloom) and bump (so the pips read as engraved).
  • Opposite faces sum to seven. It's drawn larger than a real 5/8 in die so it stays legible at table scale.

Table

  • 4 × 8 ft, 30 in off the floor, with a brushed gold-dark metal trim and slim dark legs.
  • The top is navy lacquer with fine grain and the hero's 96 px pinstripes. Its gloss is tied to the camera: matte while the camera looks straight down, so the hero reads as a flat field, then clear-coated once the camera is looking along the table. Without that, the lamp's reflection blooms into a white blob behind the headline.
  • The inlay and center line are thin HDR gold strips that trace on during the crane. The ΣΧ monogram is its own layer and fades in.
  • The floor has a faint gold grid that fades out from under the table.

Cups

  • Frosted glass (transmission), empty, with a lit gold rim. On weak devices, swap the glass for an opaque cream material; transmission costs an extra render pass.

Light and reflections

  • The environment map is built in code from drei Lightformers: a navy void, one low softbox behind the far end, a cool side panel and a gold strip. Reflections stay navy and gold; a stock "room" environment turns the lacquer grey.
  • One neutral, slightly cool key spot, placed off-axis, with soft shadows. The warmth comes only from the gold.
  • Bloom threshold 0.9, so only the gold glows. Its strength ramps from 0.25 at the hero to 0.75 once the table is revealed.

The thrower (mockups 13 and 14)

  • Built in code: each body part is a loft of rings (the blue contour lines) over a faint rim-lit shell. In 14, the rings swell into muscles. There's no model file, so the CSP stays as it is.
  • A plain joint hierarchy is posed from keyframes (idle, wind-up, release, follow-through, fist pump). Two-bone IK keeps the feet planted as the hips drop and rise.
  • The die rides in the hand until release. The launch velocity is then solved from the release point, so gravity carries it exactly into the cup.
  • Section 8 breaks down every effect, including two ways to build the figure for production.

Motion details

  • The flight trail re-samples the same deterministic path slightly in the past, so it needs no stored state.
  • A gentle pointer parallax after the intro, and no camera roll ever.

Two ways to build the thrower

A · Generated in code (what 13 and 14 do)

  • No assets, no licences, no 3D software. Every proportion is a number, which is how 14 got jacked: same code, different table.
  • The CSP stays exactly as it is.
  • About 39 draw calls for the figure (lines, borders and shell for each part), because each part moves on its own. That's fine on a laptop. On phones, drop the shell first.
  • The motion is hand-keyed, so it looks stylised rather than motion-captured.

B · A rigged body with a contour shader

  • A muscular base mesh, sculpted or from a CC0 or licensed library, rigged to a standard skeleton. Throw and celebration clips come from motion capture (Mixamo has several; confirm its licence for a public site).
  • No line geometry: a shader draws the contours from world height (fract(y × density)) plus the fresnel rim, so any mesh gets the same look in two draw calls.
  • Load it with drei's useGLTF and useAnimations. An untextured GLB served from the site keeps the CSP unchanged; embedded textures would need blob: in img-src.
  • It needs Blender for retargeting and clean-up: 2–4 extra days, and someone comfortable in it.

Recommendation: ship A first. It already exists, it's easy to tune, and it carries no asset or licence risk. Move to B only if you want lifelike motion or a library of animations, such as different celebrations or idle loops.

Line weight

WebGL draws lines 1 device pixel wide everywhere, so on a 3× phone screen the contours are hairlines. That suits a hologram. For bolder lines, three's LineSegments2 and LineMaterial draw true screen-space widths at roughly three times the vertex cost; route B's shader controls the width directly.

9 · Accessibility, performance, security

What it has to get right

Accessibility

  • All words stay HTML. The canvas is aria-hidden; the HUD is decorative.
  • The sequence runs about 3.4 seconds, under WCAG 2.2.2's five-second limit for moving content. It's skippable ("Skip past the toss"), and the cue is a real button for keyboard users.
  • Reduced motion jumps straight to the landed frame. No crane, no trail, no shake, no drifting dust. The app's MotionConfig reducedMotion="user" doesn't cover a WebGL scene, so DieTableHero checks useReducedMotion() itself.
  • The page never locks or hijacks the scrollbar.
  • The thrower follows reduced motion too: he appears fully printed, with no flicker, scan band or code rain, and the page shows the landed state instead of the throw.

Security (the site's CSP)

  • The current policy works as-is: three, R3F and post-processing ship in our bundle (script-src 'self'); textures come from canvases; the environment map is generated in code, with no HDR download.
  • Self-host JetBrains Mono through @fontsource (font-src 'self').
  • The policy would need changing only for runtime physics ('wasm-unsafe-eval'), GLB textures (blob:) or a CDN-hosted Draco decoder. The plan avoids all three.
